# Search and discovery (feature 29)

Two things share this feature: how staff find records, and how customers find a business.

## Staff search (the command palette)

`Ctrl/⌘ + K` opens the palette. After two characters it asks `GET /api/staff/search?q=` and shows customers, appointments, services, team members, invoices and campaigns next to the existing pages and actions. Choosing a result opens it: a customer's profile, the schedule on the appointment's day, a new booking for the service, the team page, the customer an invoice belongs to, or the campaign.

- **PostgreSQL only.** Matching uses `pg_trgm` (substring, phone digits and typos) and prefix full text (`to_tsvector('simple', …)`), on GIN indexes that start with `tenant_id` (`btree_gin`). No outside search service.
- **One definer function.** Under forced row-level security the planner will not use a GIN index for `ILIKE`, `%`, `<%` or `@@` (those operators are not leakproof), so the app role scanned every row of the business: 40 ms at 120,000 customers and growing with the table. `kalendr_staff_search` runs as the table owner, where the indexes work, and fences itself. The business comes from the `app.tenant_id` setting that `withTenant` made, never from a parameter. With no business set it returns nothing. It is not on a REVOKE list because the web app calls it, like `kalendr_resolve_tenant`.
- **Permissions.** The server decides which kinds a role may find (`searchKindsFor` in `src/lib/search.ts`), mirroring the screens: customers and appointments need a booking, status, payment or records permission; invoices need `invoices.issue` or `reports.revenue`; campaigns need `campaigns.manage`; a read-only account finds nothing. A team member sees only their own customers and appointments; one with no linked profile sees none.
- **Text handling.** Control characters are removed (a NUL byte makes PostgreSQL refuse text), `%` and `_` are escaped, and the full-text query is built only from letters and digits. The search text is never logged; the route logs its name and a request id.
- **Speed.** Measured on the scratch database, as the app role, with 100,000 customers, appointments and invoices and 20,000 services in one business: a name, phone, email, appointment reason, invoice number or service answers in 20 to 35 ms with all six kinds searched; a very common two-word name ("Asha Rao", thousands of fuzzy matches) took about 150 ms. `staff-search.integration.test.ts` seeds that volume, asserts every matcher uses its GIN index (`EXPLAIN`, no `Seq Scan`), and fails if a search takes over 600 ms.

## Discovery (search engines, link previews, directory)

- **Listing switch.** `tenants.listed` is off until the owner turns it on in Settings, Find and share. `tenants.seo` holds the owner's page title and description (both optional, 70 and 160 characters). Saving checks what the screen last saw (`version_conflict` otherwise), is refused in a read-only support session, and writes an activity row with field names only.
- **When a page is indexed.** The business is listed, switched on, not paused by an operator, and its plan has not lapsed (trial ended, cancelled past its period, failed payment past the 7-day grace). Everything else carries `noindex`. `planHasLapsed` (TypeScript) and `kalendr_subscription_serves` (SQL) are one rule, tested against each other.
- **Always `noindex`:** the booking flow at `/book/<business>` (the page at `/b/<business>` is what gets listed), a service page with a chosen time or any other booking state in its address, classes, manage links, the portal, check-in, queue, payment and similar links. The private areas also get an `X-Robots-Tag` header from `next.config.ts` and a `Disallow` in `robots.txt`. `/book/` itself stays crawlable so a crawler can read the `noindex`.
- **Page tags.** Business, team member, service and team pages use `pageMetadata`: an absolute title ("Book an appointment at Smile Dental, Andheri West" unless the owner wrote their own), description, canonical address (the verified own domain when there is one), Open Graph and Twitter tags.
- **Share picture.** `src/app/b/[clinicSlug]/opengraph-image.tsx` draws the business's name, city, logo and accent colour at 1200 by 630. The logo is read straight from the file store (PNG or JPEG only, under 1.5 MB). Cached for an hour.
- **Structured data.** `businessStructuredData` (`src/lib/structured-data.ts`) builds schema.org JSON-LD: `Dentist`, `MedicalClinic`, `BeautySalon` and so on, address, phone, opening hours, services with prices, and `AggregateRating` only from public, not-hidden reviews and only once there are enough to average. `jsonLdScript` escapes `<`, `>`, `&` and the line separators, so a business named `</script>` cannot break out of the tag.
- **Sitemap.** `sitemap.ts` with `generateSitemaps`: 10,000 pages per file, listed from `kalendr_sitemap_entries` (slugs and keys only), each address canonical. `robots.ts` names every file.
- **Share kit.** Settings, Find and share: one booking link per place (Instagram, WhatsApp, Google Business Profile, email signature, poster), each tagged with `utm_source`, so reports can tell them apart; an email signature line; steps for adding the link to a Google Business Profile; and a printable A4 or A5 poster at `/print/poster` with a QR code (`uqr`) in the business's colour whose link carries `utm_source=poster`.
- **At scale.** Measured as the app role with 100,000 listed businesses, each with a place, a service and a team member (`discovery.integration.test.ts` seeds that and fails past the limits): the first directory page about 100 ms, a deep page or a name search about 0.5 s, a filtered list 30 ms, the filter list 0.4 s, one sitemap file of 10,000 pages 0.45 s and the deepest file 1.3 s. The sitemap files and the directory filters are kept in each web process for ten minutes (`KALENDR_DISCOVERY_CACHE_SECONDS`, 0 turns it off), so crawlers cost the database one read per window. Real directories are a tiny fraction of this size.
- **Directory.** `/explore`: listed, running businesses by city and type, with search (name, description and place, typos on the name). Server-rendered, works without script, 60 requests a minute per address. Filtered and paged lists are `noindex`.

## Known limits

- The sitemap lists a business's own domain addresses, but a crawler only trusts a sitemap that lives on the same host. A business on its own domain needs its own sitemap submitted to Search Console; `/sitemap` is not served on custom hosts yet.
- Google may take days to show a Book button after the link is added to a Google Business Profile. Nothing here can speed that up, and Reserve with Google is not built.
- The share picture draws the logo only when it is PNG or JPEG; a WebP logo falls back to the first letter of the name.
- Search matches what is stored in the database: encrypted fields (guardian contacts, notes' sealed parts) are not searchable.
- Listing is available on every plan, so the listing switch has no plan gate. A business whose plan has lapsed is hidden from search engines whatever the switch says.
- The share picture route and `/explore` are the public routes that run a query per request. `/explore` is rate-limited (60 a minute per address); the share picture relies on Next's one-hour cache per business and answers 404, uncached, for a name that is not a running business. It has no rate limit of its own because reading the request address would make it uncacheable.
- Everything was run on PostgreSQL 17.11 (the scratch server). `pg_trgm` and `btree_gin` are ordinary contrib extensions in 16 to 18, and nothing here uses a feature newer than 16, but 16 and 18 were not run.
- No outside tool has checked the structured data (Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator need a public address) or the WhatsApp preview. The markup and the tags are tested for shape and escaping; the owner should paste a live link into both once.
- The 150 ms case above is dominated by fuzzy matching on very common names; a minimum of three characters for fuzzy matching is the next lever if it shows up in real use.
